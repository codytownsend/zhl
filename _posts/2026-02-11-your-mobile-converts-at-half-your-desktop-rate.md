## Why The Mobile Gap Is Normal—But Not Acceptable {#the-gap}

Some gap between desktop and mobile is expected. Desktop shoppers are often further down the funnel: they're researching at work, comparing tabs, buying the higher-consideration item. Mobile is browsing, discovering, killing time in line.

But "some gap" and "half" are very different things.

- **Desktop 3.1% / mobile 2.4%:** normal. Nothing to see here.
- **Desktop 3.1% / mobile 1.5%:** you're leaving a fortune on the table.

The second one is what we see most often. And it hurts more than it looks, because the majority of your paid traffic is landing on the mobile experience. You're optimizing headlines and creative to send people to the version of your store you look at the least.

## How To Measure Your Own Mobile Gap {#measure}

You don't need a fancy tool. You need to stop looking at the blended number.

### Step 1: Pull the device split

In GA4, go to Reports, then add "Device category" as the dimension and "Session conversion rate" (or purchases / sessions) as the metric. Look at the last 30 to 90 days. Write down three numbers: desktop CR, mobile CR, and the percentage of sessions that are mobile.

If you're on Shopify, the analytics also let you segment sessions and conversion by device. Either works. You just want the honest picture.

### Step 2: Calculate what the gap is worth

Take your mobile sessions per month, multiply by the difference between your desktop and mobile conversion rate, then multiply by AOV. That's your rough monthly ceiling if mobile performed like desktop.

### Step 3: Shop your own store on your actual phone

Not the desktop preview shrunk down. Your phone, on cellular data, not office wifi. Buy something. Go all the way through checkout.

Count the friction. How many taps to add to cart? Does a popup ambush you before you've seen the product? Can you read the price without zooming? Does the sticky header eat a third of the screen? This five-minute exercise finds more problems than most audits.

## The 6 Things Quietly Killing Mobile Conversions {#killers}

Nearly every mobile gap we diagnose comes down to some combination of these six. None of them require a rebuild.

### 1. The hero eats the entire first screen

On desktop your hero looks cinematic. On a phone, a full-height hero image plus a tall sticky header means the shopper has to scroll before they can tell what you sell. On a product page, it means the price and add-to-cart are buried below the fold.

Every extra scroll before the core buying information is a chance to lose someone.

### 2. No sticky add-to-cart

On mobile, product pages are long. By the time someone reads the description, checks reviews, and looks at the images, the buy button is three screens up. If they have to scroll back to purchase, some won't.

A persistent sticky add-to-cart bar—price and button always visible at the bottom—is one of the highest-ROI mobile changes you can make.

### 3. Tap targets built for a mouse

Variant swatches crammed together, quantity steppers the size of a grain of rice, "close" buttons you can barely hit. If a shopper accidentally taps the wrong size or can't dismiss your popup, that's friction you designed in.

<aside class="callout callout-warning">
    <div class="callout-header">
        <span class="callout-icon">⚠️</span>
        <span class="callout-label">Warning</span>
    </div>
    <p>If any interactive element on your product page is smaller than roughly 44 x 44 pixels, you're asking thumbs to do a mouse's job. Fat-finger errors read as friction, and friction reads as "I'll do this later."</p>
</aside>

### 4. Popups that ambush instead of invite

A popup that fires two seconds after landing, covers the whole screen, and has a close button hidden in the corner is a bounce machine on mobile. You're interrupting someone before they've even seen the product they clicked your ad to find.

Delay it. Trigger on intent or scroll depth. Make it easy to dismiss. Capturing an email means nothing if you lose the session to get it.

### 5. Slow, heavy images

We wrote a whole piece on site speed, so we'll keep this short: mobile shoppers are often on worse connections than you are. Oversized images and autoplay video punish them specifically. Fast desktop, slow mobile is one of the most common patterns we see.

### 6. A checkout that fights the thumb

Tiny form fields, no numeric keyboard for the phone number, no address autocomplete, express payment options buried below the fold. Every unnecessary keystroke on a phone is a reason to abandon. This is where a lot of "added to cart but never bought" mobile sessions die.

## Fixing The Mobile Product Page {#pdp}

The product page is where mobile traffic converts or leaks, so it deserves its own attention. Here's the order of operations we use.

### Load the buying area first

On mobile, the first screen after the main image should get the shopper to a decision fast: product title, price, star rating, variant selector, add to cart. Everything else—full description, reviews, recommendations, story content—can come after.

> If a shopper can see the product, understand the price, and tap add-to-cart within the first screen or two, you've won the hardest part of the mobile battle.

### Add a sticky buy bar

As they scroll into details and reviews, keep the price and add-to-cart pinned to the bottom of the screen. They should never have to hunt for the way to buy.

### Make variants obvious and tappable

Big, clearly-labeled swatches or buttons. Show which option is selected. Show the price update if variants are priced differently. Never make someone guess whether their tap registered.

### Move trust up, not away

Reviews, guarantees, and shipping info build confidence—but on mobile you can't show everything at once. Surface a compact version high (star rating next to the title, a one-line shipping/returns note near the button) and keep the full detail further down.

## Real World Example: 1.1% To 2.0% On Mobile {#real-world-example}

A supplements brand on Shopify reached out with the usual story: strong Meta spend, blended conversion rate around 1.9%, and a nagging feeling that their ad account was "getting worse." It wasn't the ad account.

The device split told the story:

- Desktop conversion rate: 3.2%
- Mobile conversion rate: 1.1%
- Mobile share of traffic: 74%

Three-quarters of their paid traffic was hitting a mobile experience converting at a third of desktop. We ran a focused four-week sprint, mobile only:

### First-screen triage

- Cut the full-height hero on the product page in half
- Moved price, rating, and add-to-cart above the fold
- Pushed the ingredient story and long-form content below

### Sticky buying area

- Added a persistent add-to-cart bar with live price
- Enlarged variant and quantity controls for thumbs

### Killed the friction

- Delayed the popup to fire on exit-intent and deep scroll instead of on load
- Enabled express checkout buttons high on the cart
- Fixed the checkout to trigger the numeric keyboard and address autocomplete

## The Bottom Line: Stop Optimizing The Version You Never See

Here's the trap. You design on desktop. You review on desktop. You screenshot for the deck on desktop. Then you spend 70% of your ad budget sending strangers to the mobile store you barely look at.

Mobile isn't a smaller version of your site. It's the version most of your customers actually experience. Treat it like the main event, because for your revenue, it is.

> If your store feels even slightly annoying on your own phone, remember: your customer doesn't have your patience, your wifi, or your reason to forgive it.
